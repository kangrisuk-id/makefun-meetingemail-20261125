# Candidates — The Meeting That Should Have Been an Email

44 candidate lines, each scored against the judge's five criteria **in the brief's order**
(each /5, total /25):

1. **On-topic gate** — one line, plainly a joke, unmistakably about a meeting that should have been an email
2. **The laugh** — lands on its own, no setup, the turn arriving by the last word
3. **Surprise** — a fresh angle, not the obvious complaint about length or "could have been an email"
4. **Economy** — every word earns its place, no throat-clearing, no second sentence
5. **Recognition** — names something true about meetings people have actually sat through

## Knockout tournament (top 8 seeds, all 24/25; decided on the first criterion where they differ)

Quarter-finals:
- #5 reply-all with chairs beat #22 quorum — criterion 2: "a reply-all with chairs" is the harder final-word laugh.
- #24 read-aloud document beat #11 take it offline — criterion 5: the shared-document read-aloud is the most universally sat-through ritual.
- #34 premiere beat #14 recurring subscription — criterion 3: the premiere frame is the fresher angle.
- #44 expensive first draft beat #23 follow-up loop — criterion 3: the record → transcribe → email pipeline is the angle nobody expects.

Semi-finals:
- #24 beat #5 — criterion 2: the doubled "grown adults" repetition lands harder than the chairs tag.
- #44 beat #34 — criterion 1: #44 names the email itself being sent; #34 only implies it.

Final:
- **#44 beat #24** — criterion 1 first: #44 is unmistakably about the email (the transcript literally gets emailed), then criterion 3: "the email's expensive first draft" is the surprise turn, arriving on the last word (criterion 2), and the recording/transcript ritual is instantly recognisable (criterion 5).

## Winner

> We recorded it, transcribed it, and emailed the transcript — the meeting was just the email's expensive first draft.

**24/25** — 116 characters (gate: 1–200). Device: pipeline-reversal — the meeting demoted to a rough draft of the email it should have been.
